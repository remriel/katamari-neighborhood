# Eon Roll — From the First Matter to a Living Cosmos

## 1. The game we’re making

A **mobile-first, finite, 30–45 minute adventure** spanning six distinct gameplay styles:

**Atoms → cells → organisms → villages → civilizations → planets.**

The player is a mysterious, persistent seed that gathers Earth’s material and history into itself. It begins microscopic on the young Earth, develops into a moving ecosystem, carries thriving human settlements, and eventually becomes a fantastical cosmic entity.

The central promise is: **“Everything you gathered helped you become this.”**

Decisions already locked:

- **Grounded fantasy:** recognizable historical eras and scientific ideas, with clearly fantastical player abilities.
- **Six distinct games:** each era changes the player’s verbs, controls, decisions, and failure conditions.
- **One persistent entity:** collected material remains part of its history and layered body.
- **Build and connect during the human eras:** villages and civilizations remain living communities.
- **Checkpoint adventure:** short retries, automatic saves, and no campaign-wide countdown.
- **Replay through different builds:** adaptations change how later chapters play.
- **A definite ending:** complete the cosmic finale and inspect the finished entity; no endless mode.

The opening treats atoms as materials already present when Earth formed. The emergence of life uses approximate dates and acknowledges uncertainty. Agriculture appears around the past 12,000 years; the planetary chapter is explicitly a speculative future. These distinctions follow [NASA’s early-life overview](https://science.nasa.gov/astrobiology/learning-resources/alp/sources-of-lifes-building-blocks/) and [Smithsonian’s account of farming and settlement](https://humanorigins.si.edu/human-characteristics/humans-change-world).

## 2. The six-era campaign

Target **about 38 minutes of active play**, plus brief transformations and adaptation choices. Each chapter follows the same pacing structure: a safe introduction, two increasingly demanding objectives, and a memorable climax.

| Era | Distinct gameplay and controls | Progression and chapter finish |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Matter — young Earth, approximately 4.5 billion years ago** · **4 min** | **Magnetic bonding puzzle.** Drag the seed through a drifting chemical field, rotate its cluster, and tap a polarity pulse to attract or repel particles. Matching bonding sites matter more than simply collecting everything. Use simplified, clearly labeled molecular recipes. | Assemble three stable clusters based on water, methane, and ammonia. Heat zones and impacts disrupt unfinished bonds without deleting collected particles. Finish by guiding the assembled material into a cooling pool during a short bombardment sequence. The cluster becomes the core of a membrane. |
| **2. Cells — early oceans** · **6 min** | **Fluid survival and resource management.** Steer through currents; time membrane openings to ingest nutrients while excluding toxins. Energy powers movement and a purge pulse. Three nutrient categories support membrane repair, movement, and growth. | Recruit three complementary symbionts and complete three metabolic cycles. Finish by crossing a hostile environmental plume using the functions you assembled. The membrane opens into a multicellular body. |
| **3. Organisms — complex life through changing habitats** · **7 min** | **Creature action and adaptation.** Swim, dash, latch onto compatible organisms, and evade larger predators. Attached fins, shells, limbs, and plants alter the moving body’s silhouette and behavior. Progress through shallow water, land, and a cold habitat. | Acquire the functional adaptations needed for all three habitats. Required traversal abilities are guaranteed; optional build choices improve them. Finish with a migration sequence involving pursuit and environmental hazards. Human communities then begin settling on the living landscape you carry. |
| **4. Villages — farming and settlement** · **7 min** | **Spatial logistics on a mobile settlement.** Tap destinations to plan travel and drag buildings into connected slots on the entity. Farms, storage, homes, and workshops form visible supply chains. Limit resources to food, timber, and stone. | Establish three functioning production chains and support eight homes. Route choices trade travel time against resource opportunities. Finish by preparing supplies and carrying the settlement across a dangerous river crossing. Buildings and inhabitants remain attached afterward. |
| **5. Civilizations — cities, industry, and global cooperation** · **8 min** | **Network strategy.** Zoom out to a regional map, connect cities, balance energy and goods, and reroute overloaded links. Cities continue operating on the accumulated landscape. The chapter advances through trade, industrial power, and planetary cooperation. | Connect three major regions, establish resilient energy and supply networks, and construct an orbital project. Finish by maintaining essential services through a cascading network disruption, then completing the launch. Depict multiple cultures developing in parallel rather than one universal historical ladder. |
| **6. Planets — speculative future** · **6 min** | **Orbital capture and momentum control.** Use thrust, braking, trajectory previews, and gravity assists. Begin with debris and moons, then approach planetary bodies. Matching velocity and maintaining stability replace surface rolling as the central skill. | Capture rocky, icy, and gas-giant world archetypes into the entity’s gravitationally bound outer structure. Complete a final 30-second stabilization sequence. End with a cinematic pullback and a cutaway revealing the original microscopic core inside everything collected. |

**Transitions are rewards.** Use a short continuous camera move, a recognizable fragment of the previous body, a new soundscape, and an immediate demonstration of the next mechanic. Advance historical time at milestones and chapter transitions, not through a continuously racing date counter.

Each era has its own camera and input mapping. Do not force village construction or orbital flight to behave like the existing thumbstick rolling game.

## 3. What makes it engaging and replayable

### Moment-to-moment rewards

- Aim for a useful interaction every **5–10 seconds** and a small goal, discovery, or decision every **45–90 seconds**.
- Make pickups visibly travel into the entity, occupy a meaningful location, and change its shape, function, or available options.
- Use responsive sound, small bursts of motion, and brief combo feedback. Reserve larger celebrations for meaningful discoveries and chapter completion.
- Reveal future opportunities before they become usable: a predator too large to approach, a distant settlement, an inactive city connection, or a planet requiring more momentum control.
- Keep the main HUD to **current objective, era resource, and progress**. Put history details and the collection archive behind an optional panel.

### Builds that carry through history

At the end of each of the first five chapters, choose one adaptation from three persistent development paths:

| Path | Playstyle | How it changes later eras |
|---|---|---|
| **Cohesion** | Resilient, stable, forgiving | Stronger membranes, better impact resistance, safer settlement transport, more resilient networks, and greater tolerance of tidal stress. |
| **Motion** | Fast, agile, technically demanding | Better bursts and evasions, faster travel, shorter transport delays, and more effective orbital thrust. |
| **Symbiosis** | Efficient, cooperative, chain-focused | Better nutrient use, helpful companion behavior, more productive supply chains, shared network reserves, and biosphere-supported recovery. |

Every choice adds one rank to its path and a visible physical motif: protective structures, streamlined appendages, or flourishing biological communities.

Initial balancing defaults:

- Cohesion adds **15% hazard tolerance per rank**, capped at 60%.
- Motion improves the era’s movement or transport capability by **10% per rank**, capped at 40%.
- Symbiosis improves the era’s useful resource output by **15% per rank**, capped at 60%.
- Each path’s first rank also unlocks a contextual behavior: a recoverable shield, an additional movement burst or rerouting option, or a cooperative resource link.
- All mandatory objectives remain solvable with any build. Adaptations create advantages and alternate routes, never required keys.

Show the next chapter’s concrete benefit on the choice screen. The player should understand why choosing a membrane upgrade now will matter later.

### Replay structure and failure

- First playthrough uses a curated campaign layout.
- Subsequent runs choose between **two authored layout variants per era**, using a saved seed. Variants change routes, hazards, and resource placement while preserving objective solvability.
- Replay resets the build, encouraging different combinations. Completed discoveries remain in a permanent archive.
- Unlock chapter selection after finishing the campaign.
- Failure returns the player to the latest safe checkpoint or restarts the local set piece. Keep the accumulated entity and collected-item ledger.
- Collected IDs cannot award duplicate rewards after a retry.
- Use no global lives, campaign-wide countdown, compulsory grinding, daily streaks, or endless continuation.

The final screen shows the assembled entity, its development path, major discoveries, and a replay option. A layer viewer lets the player trace the journey back to the first molecular cluster.

## 4. Implementation and presentation

### Preserve the current work and rebuild the campaign boundary

The live site is still V4. The working tree contains an **unfinished, unpublished V5** with compound-body and finite-island work.

Before implementation:

1. Preserve that complete working state in an archival Git commit/branch.
2. Continue the Earth-history redesign in the existing repository.
3. Retain useful mobile controls, asset loading, world-coordinate fixes, and compound-body data.
4. Replace the unfinished island campaign and its timer-based progression with this six-era campaign.

Keep **Vite, Three.js, and DOM interfaces**. Use the existing compound-body work as a prototype foundation, with simplified compound collision bodies for physical chapters. Rapier supports attaching multiple colliders to one rigid body, which suits the physical accumulation stages. [Rapier collider documentation](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/colliders/)

### Separate the six games cleanly

Replace the growing conditional logic in `src/main.js` with a campaign controller and six independent era modules.

Each era module must provide:

- Entry and checkpoint restoration.
- Its own simulation update and camera mode.
- Its input actions and touch/desktop mappings.
- Objective evaluation and chapter completion.
- Serializable state.
- Renderer creation and disposal.

The shared campaign state owns:

- Current era and checkpoint.
- Build choices.
- Collection ledger and entity ancestry.
- Historical timeline position.
- Run seed and save-format version.

Era modules report events such as collection, objective completion, damage, discovery, and transition requests. They must not directly modify another era’s state.

### Persistent accumulation across enormous scale changes

- Give every collected gameplay object a stable ID and parent relationship.
- Keep its ownership and history for the entire campaign.
- Use local coordinates and scale metadata for each era; never simulate nanometers and planetary distances in one floating-point coordinate space.
- Tiny earlier objects become internal layers or render proxies as the camera pulls back. Preserve their records and ancestry instead of deleting them.
- Use instancing and level-of-detail representations for the visible body.
- Preserve fixed attachment transforms through physical growth. Rebuild collision proxies only on attachment or structural changes.
- Keep terrain and environmental coordinates independent of camera movement and chunk loading, preserving the fixes already made for sliding backgrounds and recreated objects.

### Saves and art direction

- Save campaign state locally in IndexedDB at objectives, adaptation choices, chapter transitions, and periodic safe points.
- Keep a last-known-good checkpoint so an interrupted save cannot destroy the run.
- Pause on loss of focus. Support resuming partway through a chapter.
- Use a coherent, richly textured illustrated-diorama style across all eras, with distinct palettes and soundscapes.
- Generate original concept art, textures, and suitable cutouts with ImageGen. Use production-ready GLB assets for substantial tactile organisms, buildings, and landmarks.
- Do not ship placeholder geometry or reuse modern neighborhood objects in prehistoric scenes.
- Reuse existing art only where its subject and period genuinely fit.
- Provide reduced motion, mute, large touch targets, and color-plus-shape cues for chemistry and network states.

## 5. Delivery and acceptance

Implement in this order:

1. **Campaign shell, save system, collection ancestry, and era-module interface.**
2. **Atoms and cells**, including the first complete transformation and adaptation choice.
3. **Organisms and villages**, including compound-body movement and working logistics.
4. **Civilizations and planets**, including the finale.
5. **Era artwork, sound, tutorials, checkpoint recovery, and final layer viewer.**
6. **One production build, required hosting packaging, GitHub synchronization, and public deployment of the complete campaign.**

The release target is the full six-era journey. Do not publish an incomplete collection of era placeholders as the finished game.

Continue the user’s **build-once-publish** workflow: fix build or publication blockers, then publish. No automated gameplay suite, browser playtest, performance audit, or repeated validation unless subsequently requested.

Manual acceptance after publication should cover:

- Each era has a clearly different gameplay loop and can be completed with touch controls.
- A first complete run targets 30–45 minutes.
- Every build path can finish every chapter.
- Failures and reloads preserve collected history and restore a usable checkpoint.
- Earlier objects remain represented through later transformations.
- Growth and era transitions do not move existing scenery unexpectedly.
- Village and civilization systems reward building and connecting.
- The planetary finale produces a definite ending and a coherent final entity.
- A new run changes build possibilities and layout variants without becoming endless.

**Defaults:** same public Sites project and private GitHub repository; single-player; local saves; no accounts, backend economy, multiplayer, or monetization. Historical dates are approximate framing, and supernatural accumulation, transformations, and the cosmic finale are presented as fantasy.
